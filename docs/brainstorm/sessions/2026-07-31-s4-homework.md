## The finding: everything asked for is built, and three parts of it are broken

HW-1 shipped on 2026-07-29, two days ago. Against the brief:

| Asked for | Status |
|---|---|
| Teacher sets homework for a class | ✅ two surfaces — My Day class card, and the period card |
| Next day, mark who did and who didn't | ✅ *"Everyone did it"* one tap, then a tap-to-cycle roster |
| Admin: school / class / subject completion | ✅ plus named repeat-missers with working actions |
| Admin: drill to one student — which homework, when | 🔴 **built, wired into the web client, and called by nothing** |
| A screen for marking yesterday's homework | ✅ as a block on My Day — and the real gap is elsewhere |

So, as in sessions 2 and 3, the useful conversation was *what is wrong with it*.

## 🔴 Four verified defects

**1 · Friday's homework cannot be checked on Monday.**
`ClassroomService.my_day` builds the checking queue from **exactly one day**: `date == today − 1`.
So Friday's homework never appears on Monday — `yesterday` is Sunday. Any day the teacher skips
for any reason loses that homework from the only surface that offers to check it, **permanently**.
And `due_date` is ignored by the queue entirely: a project due next Monday is offered for checking
tomorrow, and its actual deadline passes with no prompt.

**The blast radius is a judgement about a person.** `/dashboard/homework`'s *"Overdue, unchecked"*
column counts homework the teacher **was never shown**. The most likely reason a school's checking
rate looks bad right now is this query, not its teachers.

**2 · A child who was absent is marked as not having done it.**
The check sheet lists the whole roster with no idea who was in the room when the homework was set
— attendance recorded it, on that date, in the same database. So a child off sick on Tuesday is
flagged `not_done` on Wednesday, lands on *"Students who keep missing it"*, and **their guardian
is reminded about work they were never given.**

Same family as *not-captured is never a zero*, pointed at a child: **absent is not a refusal.**

**3 · The founder's drill-down is built and unreachable.**
`GET /homework/student/{id}` returns exactly *"which homework and when he missed"* — every item
with date, subject, text and verdict. `schoolApi.studentHomework` exists in the web client.
**Nothing calls it.** The report card shows per-subject counts only. `D-31` is a component away.

**4 · A substitute cannot check homework, though the model expects them to.**
`HomeworkCheck.checked_by_member_id` was added with the comment *"a substitute may check a
colleague's homework"*. But `_can_capture` allows only an admin or the class-subject's assigned
teacher; it never consults `period_substitutions`, unlike `assert_can_take_class`, which DASH3
extended for exactly this. The teacher covering 7-A can take the register and log the topic, and
cannot mark the homework sitting in front of her. A loose end from session 3.

## And a fifth, quieter one

**"Partial" is worth nothing here and half a topic next door.** `HomeworkService` computes
`completion = done / (done + not_done + partial)` — a child who did most of it scores **identically
to one who did nothing**. `insights/syllabus.py` counts a partially covered topic as **0.5**. Same
word, two arithmetics, one product (`Q-40`).

Fourth instance of the pattern: three definitions of "absent for a day", two of "syllabus
covered", two of "who is free right now", now two of "partial".

## The founder's screen, reconciled

*"I guess we need an additional screen to mark the homework for student done."*

The My Day block already does this and is in the right place — she is on that screen anyway and it
costs one tap (P1v2). **What has never existed is somewhere for the pile**: Friday's homework, the
two mornings she skipped, the project whose deadline is today.

So `S-84`: **My Day keeps yesterday at one tap; the new screen is the backlog** — everything
unchecked past its deadline, oldest first, with its age. Two surfaces, two jobs, the same
discipline session 3 applied to the timesheet's three altitudes. It needs defect 1 fixed first, or
it has nothing to list.

## The idea worth keeping

**`S-87` — homework load per class per day.** Six teachers each set thirty minutes, independently,
and no screen anywhere adds them up. The child has three hours; the school made six reasonable
decisions; nobody could see the total. Give the **class teacher** her class's evening (*"Tuesday —
5 subjects set homework"*) and the **admin** the outliers.

It needs no new capture — `homework_assignments` grouped by class × date. It answers the single
most common parent complaint about Indian schools, and no product shows it.

⚠️ Show the number, **legislate nothing** (`Q-42`). A cap becomes "whose turn is it to set homework
tonight", which is worse than the problem.

## What the teacher gets back — the thing that decides adoption

Setting homework pays her immediately: parents notified, P3 satisfied. **Checking pays her
nothing**, and checking is the expensive half — one sheet per assignment — with the output landing
on the principal's dashboard.

`S-89`: as she taps a name, the row says *"Kabir · 4th miss in 5"*. She is holding the notebook,
standing in front of the child. That is the cheapest intervention moment in the entire product,
and the number is already computed — it is just being sent somewhere else.

## Two fences named

- **No marks** (`S-91`). There is a real pull toward grading — she can see the work. It would turn
  a 30-second sweep into a grading session and break the one-minute budget for the capture
  teachers actually do every day. *"Did it, but all wrong"* already has a home:
  `lesson_observations`, exception-only, already feeding the growth report.
- **No quota** (`S-92`). There is no "homework should have been set" expectation today and there
  should not be one. A target on *setting* produces homework that exists for a dashboard — the
  same argument the syllabus board already makes about coverage as a performance target.

# Session 4, continued — the founder's answers

All five questions answered, three new mechanics, and one Claude proposal rejected. Seven
decisions: `D-33`–`D-39`.

## `D-33` — late completion on a working-day clock

Mark it done at any point; past a **3-day gap** it can only be recorded as **late completion**;
**Sundays and holidays dropped**.

This kills the Friday-on-Monday defect **by design rather than by patch** — nothing expires off
her screen, so there is nothing to fall off. It is a better answer than `S-83`'s query fix,
because the query fix still let a homework sit at the bottom of a list looking identical to
yesterday's.

The working-day arithmetic already exists three times over (`leave.py::_working_days`,
`calendar.py::expand_blocked_dates`, the planner's effective-teaching-days engine). Use one
(`S-96`) — a fourth would be the fifth instance of this codebase's signature bug. And the
threshold wants to be an org setting, default 3: a boarding school and a day school will not agree.

## 🔴 The one thing to settle before building it — `Q-44`

**A long gap has two causes and only one of them is about the child.**

| Cause | Whose fact | Already computable |
|---|---|---|
| the child handed it in late | the child's | needs the new verdict |
| the teacher collected on time and recorded it Friday | **the record's** | `checked_at` vs deadline — exists today |

Forcing "late completion" on every student because the *teacher* was slow writes something untrue
about a child into their permanent history — the exact mistake `not_checked` was invented to
prevent, and this module's best existing idea.

Recommendation: past the threshold the default wording is **"recorded late"**; the teacher can
still say a specific child genuinely handed it in late; and the teacher's own lateness keeps
flowing to the admin separately — which is what `D-38` asks for anyway. It is one word on a screen
and a permanent fact in a child's record.

## `D-34` / `D-35` — the absent child, answered better than either option offered

Neither "auto-exclude" nor "teacher's call". A third state: **the days are dropped, the homework
is carried as pending, and when he returns the teacher decides** — do the backlog, or be set fresh
work. The parent sees it **yellow and pending**, so they know class was missed and work is
outstanding.

Two things it needs:

- **A waive** (`S-98`). *"Give him new work instead"* is a real outcome with nowhere to go today,
  and without it the pending items — and the parent's yellow — never clear.
- **Invisibility to the streak and the red list** (`S-102`). `_streak` already skips
  `not_checked` for exactly this reason (*"the teacher's gap is not the student's"*); carried
  needs identical treatment, or a child off sick for a week tops *"Students who keep missing it"*
  and their guardian gets reminded — defect §4.2 returning through a different door.

Worth noting: **yellow is the right colour and it is already the product's word for this.** `D-02`
uses yellow for *known and being handled*, red for *nobody has explained this*. A parent now reads
one palette across attendance and homework.

## `D-36` — the My Day block goes, and Claude was wrong about why it should stay

`S-84` argued for keeping the one-tap block on My Day because she is on that screen anyway.
**Rejected.** The founder's description of the actual workflow settles it: *"she collects the
notebooks and after the class completes she checks and marks as done in the app."* That is a desk
activity with a stack of books — minutes, not seconds. Optimising it for a thumb between periods
was solving a problem that does not exist.

So: no block, a **button**, and a Homework screen with three levels — classes × homework by day →
the check sheet → by student and by day.

**One thing decides whether this works: the button must carry a count** (`S-100`). *"Homework"* is
a button nobody presses; *"Homework · 4 to check"* is a to-do. Moving a daily habit behind an
unlabelled button is how it becomes a monthly one.

And a happy consequence: **the by-student view is the endpoint that already exists and nothing
calls** (`S-86`). The teacher's by-student view and the admin's `D-31` drill-down are one
component, built once (`S-101`).
