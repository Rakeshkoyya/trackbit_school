## The thing nobody knows is already built

`RecommendationsService._generate` reads the class's band distribution and materialises, per
class-subject per day, with **zero teacher setup**: ≤ 2 class-wide checks, **+ 1 richer check when
there are C-band children in the room**, **+ ≤ 1 targeted check per intervention student, by name**.
The teacher confirms *"class did it ✓"* and taps only the misses, which land per child in
`check_results`.

So the daily differentiation the founder is asking for **is running today**, in every seeded school,
and no screen in the product ever says so. `S-172`: the programme should *feed* this generator, not
grow a parallel "today's activity for Kabir" list — a teacher who sees the same instruction from two
systems trusts neither.

## 🔴 Five verified defects

**1 · Every intervention in every real school is created unassigned.**
`create_intervention` takes its assignee from `school_classes.class_teacher_member_id`
(`assessments.py:546-551`) — and **no screen sets that field**: `createClass` doesn't send it,
`updateClass` doesn't exist (verified in session 1, still true). The founder's *"a teacher is
assigned and it is her responsibility"* is already in the code and has never once worked.

**2 · An intervention can never be finished — and it keeps generating work forever.**
`Intervention.status` allows `active|achieved|dropped`. There are exactly two intervention
endpoints, `POST` and `GET` (`endpoints/assessments.py:249-271`). **Nothing updates the status.**
And `recommendations._intervention_students` filters `status == 'active'`, so **a goal achieved in
July keeps injecting a targeted check into the period card in March.** The one loop the module
exists to close is the one thing the code cannot do. *This is the fix that must land before
anything else in the module is built.*

**3 · Fourth instance of "built, wired into the client, called by nothing."**
`schoolApi.studentInterventions` exists (`school-api.ts:415`) and no component calls it. The report
card renders the tier and the tier history and never the plan attached to them. After `S-86`
(per-student homework history), `S-74` (the cover picker and the timesheet) and the fees defaulter
list, **this is now a standing packet-close check: does a screen call this?**

**4 · One letter per child, when the child is three different students.**
`categorize_from_cycle` sums **every score in the cycle** into one percentage and writes one overall
tier. A child who reads two years below grade and is fine at arithmetic gets a letter that describes
neither — and the check generator then hands him easier *maths* he doesn't need while his English
goes untouched. `student_bands.scope_skill_area_id` was built for exactly this and **has never been
written by any code path**.

**5 · A slip test can re-band a class.**
`apply_band_suggestions` offers a one-tap re-band of every child from `_latest_pcts`, which takes
each student's **most recent cycle with scores — whatever it is**. A five-mark Hindi slip test on
Tuesday can move eleven children between support tiers. Same family as `S-114`.

## The design argument of the session

**The daily log is the risk, and the answer is that the page opens already written.**

The founder's picture — *"for each student she can enter the logs today they did this and that"* —
is the natural shape and it is also how this module dies. Six children × a free textarea × five days
is thirty entries a week from a teacher with a full timetable. Week one it gets done; week three it
says "flashcards" thirty times; and by then the admin's movement report is built on noise.

But the child's week is **already recorded**, by other people, doing their normal work:

| Signal | Table |
|---|---|
| *"needs work on Reading today"* | `lesson_observations` — exception-only ratings + note |
| *"missed the C-band check"* | `check_results` |
| *"didn't do homework"* | `homework_results`, with `not_checked` kept distinct |
| *"wasn't here"* | `attendance_exceptions` |
| *"finished 2 pages unaided in evening study"* | `session_student_logs` |
| *"I spent period 7 on reading practice"* | `timesheet_entries`, `work_type='student_support'` |

So `S-164`: the child's page **opens with his week filled in**, and asks only for the one thing she
alone knows. P3 — value before data. And `S-165`: the **weekly check-in** (worked on · what changed
· what's next · ready to re-test) is the unit, the daily note the exception. Six children, five
minutes, Friday.

`Q-73` is the decision that determines whether anyone is still using this module in November.

## What this module deliberately isn't

No parent-facing tier, in any form, ever (P4 — restated because this module multiplies the surfaces
a letter could leak from one to about seven) · **no IEP or diagnostic framing** — a band is a
teaching group, and the moment a descriptor reads like a diagnosis this is a different product with
a different duty of care · **no ranking of teachers by children moved** (`S-170`, the `D-25`
timesheet rule: a log that can cost an appraisal stops being true, and the best teacher gets the
hardest children by construction) · no ranking of children — a tier never sorts a class · no test
authoring (`S-171` — the re-assessment is a `band_test`, which already exists) · no per-student photo
evidence (P5) · no second daily-work generator (`S-172`).

## What the founder settled

1. **Per subject, and that's the whole answer** (`D-75`) — *"a child good at maths will be in A band
   in maths but poor at Hindi will be assigned to C."* No overall letter survives. `Q-71` closed at
   option (a).
2. **Re-banding happens on a test — and the teacher can promote an ordinary one** (`D-76`) —
   *"there will be a separate test … and also the teacher can consider one of the normal CET or
   slip tests to mark as band exam test, and it's up to her choice."* `Q-74` closed.
3. **One owner per subject** (`D-77`). `Q-72` closed at option (a).

## The promotion idea is better than what I proposed

I argued in `Q-74` that test-only rebanding was the *weaker* option: a child who plainly reads
fluently would sit in C until the term's exam, and the decision would fall to whichever paper
happened to be set. **Promotion removes both objections.** The owner who thinks a child is ready
marks next Tuesday's slip test as the band test, and the move happens on marks she chose. Every
band row becomes evidenced **by construction**, which is what `S-178` was trying to reach by
process.

It also collapses two of the first pass's problems into one clean deletion. `apply_band_suggestions`
— re-band the class from *each child's most recent cycle, whatever it was* — was defect 5. With an
explicit promotion route there is no reason to keep an implicit one, so `S-183` deletes it rather
than patching it.

**Four rules ride along, and they are the whole of the build risk:**

| | |
|---|---|
| `S-182` | Promotion is a **flag**, never a `type` change. Re-typing a slip test to `band_test` deletes it from the exam analytics — the school would trade the test to gain the band. Same column region as `D-55`/`S-135`'s school-owned exam types: one migration or they collide. |
| `S-184` | A promoted test must be **locked** (`D-53`) — an unverified transcription must not move a child between support tiers — and its **total marks and how many sat it** are on screen. A small test **warns, never blocks** (SF-1's leave-policy shape). |
| `S-187` | A promoted test re-bands **one subject**. |
| `Q-81` | `student_bands` is append-only, so a mistaken re-band is **permanent**. Today `categorize_from_cycle` is one tap with no preview. *Claude: show the moves — "Kabir C→B, Diya B→C, 20 unchanged, 14 didn't sit it" — before it commits.* A child slipping **down** is the most consequential thing this module does and currently nobody reads his name. |

## What per-subject makes cheaper, not dearer

`categorize_from_cycle` already refuses org-wide cycles and SC-5 exams are already class × subject,
so writing the band **scoped to the cycle's subject** is a small change — and where a cycle carries
several subjects (a term exam) it groups by `subject_id` and writes **one row per subject in one
pass**: a term exam re-bands a child in every subject at once, which is what a school means by it.

And the daily check generator gets **more accurate for free**: `_generate` already runs per
class-subject, so the English period will finally hand the easier route to the children who cannot
read, instead of to whoever the blended letter caught.
